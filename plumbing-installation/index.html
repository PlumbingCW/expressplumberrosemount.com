Plumbing installation covers everything from a new kitchen faucet to a bathroom added in the basement. The pieces are the same: water supply, drain, vent and a place to put the fixture. What changes is how much of the house has to be opened up to connect them.

This page explains what we install, how a basement rough-in is finished into a bathroom, how to handle permits and licensing in Rosemount, and what decides the price. We tell you plainly where the city or the state makes the rules, because those rules are not ours to guess.

What Plumbing Installation Includes

These are the jobs we are asked to do most. Each begins with a look at what is already behind the walls.

New fixtures.

Sinks, toilets, tubs, showers and faucets, set and connected with new shutoffs where needed.

Bathroom additions.

New supply, drain and vent lines for a bath in a basement, attic or spare room.

Basement rough-in.

Drain and supply stubs set before a floor is poured or finished, or completed from existing stub-outs.

Laundry moves.

Washer boxes, standpipes and drain lines relocated to a new room.

Kitchen changes.

Moving a sink, adding a dishwasher or an island sink, or a pot filler.

Relocated lines.

Rerouting supply and drain pipes around a remodel or addition.

Rosemount Basements and Future Bath Stub-Outs

The city's 2040 plan says Rosemount added nearly 400 homes a year between 1999 and 2005. Many of those homes have basements that were roughed in for a future bathroom.

That is good news if you want to finish your basement. A stub-out is a drain pipe set in the slab, and sometimes capped supply lines nearby. Before you frame walls, we locate the stub-out, check that it lines up with where you want the toilet, shower and sink, and test the drain.

Source: City of Rosemount 2040 Plan, Chapter 4, Housing.

From Rough-In to Working Fixture

A new bathroom or kitchen move follows the same sequence. Skipping a stage causes trouble later.

1

Walk the house.

We look at the existing lines, the water heater, the main drain and where the new fixtures will go.

2

Plan the layout.

Drain slope, vent paths and supply routes are drawn up so walls and floors can be framed around them.

3

Rough in.

Pipes are set in the open framing or slab, capped and tested before they are covered.

4

Inspect and close.

Where inspection is required, it happens at the rough-in stage, before drywall and finishes go up.

5

Set fixtures and test.

Fixtures are set, connected and checked for leaks, flow and drainage.

A plumber who sees the project before the framers arrive saves you rework. Call us before the walls go up.

Permits and the City Building Office

Plumbing work in Rosemount runs through the city's Building Permits and Inspections office, which enforces plumbing along with building and mechanical codes. You can reach it at 651-322-2024.

We do not guess at fees or rules. What needs a permit, what an inspection involves and how long it takes are questions for that office. Ask before you start, especially for a new bathroom, relocated drains or anything behind a wall.

If we handle the project, we work with you on the permit steps and schedule the work around inspections. If you hire another contractor for part of it, ask who pulls the permit and who is on the hook for the inspection.

Licensed Contractor or Doing It Yourself

Minnesota licenses plumbing work, with one exception for homeowners. The honest advice is to confirm your city's rule first.

Hiring a licensed plumber.

  • The Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry says a business that contracts plumbing work must hold a plumbing contractor license.
  • That license requires a responsible master plumber, insurance and a bond.
  • You can check a license on the department's lookup page before hiring.
  • The contractor handles permits, testing and any corrections.
  • Warranty on the work comes from a company, not from you.

Homeowner doing their own work.

  • Minnesota Statutes 326B.46 lets an unlicensed person do compliant plumbing work on premises they own and occupy as a residence, unless a local ordinance forbids it.
  • The work must still meet the state plumbing standards.
  • The statute applies to your own residence, not a rental or a neighbor's house.
  • Confirm the local rule with the city office before buying parts.
  • Mistakes in drains, vents or gas are costly and sometimes dangerous.

Our view is simple. Small fixture swaps are the kind of work many owners take on. Moving drains, adding a bath or working near a water heater is where a licensed plumber earns the fee.

What Sets the Price of a Plumbing Installation

We give you a clear price before work starts. These factors drive it.

Distance from existing lines.New fixtures near the main stack are cheaper than ones far from it.
Opening floors and walls.Cutting a slab or finished wall adds labor and repair work.
Fixture selection.Premium fixtures and special valves cost more and may need more time.
Permits and inspections.Permit steps and inspection scheduling affect the timeline. Ask the city office about fees.
Condition of existing plumbing.Old drains or supply pipes may need replacement to carry the new load.

Insurance coverage varies by policy. Water damage from a failed new connection is a different matter, which is why testing before closing walls matters.
