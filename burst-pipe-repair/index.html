The First Five Minutes After a Pipe Bursts

Speed matters more than neatness. Work through these steps in order and call as soon as the water is stopped.

  • 1
    Find and close the main shutoff.Look near the water meter, the water heater or where the line enters the basement wall. Turn a round handle clockwise until it stops. A lever valve closes with a quarter turn.
  • 2
    Open taps to drain the lines.Open the lowest faucet in the house and a few upstairs taps, cold and hot. Opening them relieves pressure and lets the pipes empty so less water leaks out of the break.
  • 3
    Think about power before you step in water.If water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not wade into it. Turn off power from a dry spot, or stay out and call the electric utility or a licensed electrician.
  • 4
    Turn off the water heater.Shut off the gas control or the electric breaker so the tank does not heat while it drains. Do not touch gas controls if you smell gas. Leave the house and call from outside.
  • 5
    Call us and start photos.Tell us where the water is coming from and what you shut off. Then take photos and video of the damage before you move anything.

We handle after-hours calls through the same line, and a same-day visit is possible when the schedule allows.

Frozen Pipe Thaw: What to Do and What Never to Try

A frozen pipe has not always split yet. Thawing it carefully can save the pipe, but only if you do it the safe way.

Safe thawing.

  • Open the faucet nearest the frozen pipe so melting water has somewhere to go.
  • Warm the pipe with a hair dryer, starting near the faucet and working back toward the frozen section.
  • Wrap the pipe in towels soaked in warm water, or use a heating pad if the pipe is reachable.
  • Keep the area dry and keep the dryer's cord away from any water.
  • Leave the faucet open until the flow is steady.

Never do this.

  • Never use a torch, a lighter or any open flame. It can ignite framing and overheat the pipe.
  • Never thaw toward the freeze. Starting at the middle traps steam and pressure that can split the pipe.
  • Never ignore a pipe that is already bulging, cracked or dripping. Shut off the main and call.
  • Never leave a heat source running unattended in a wall or crawl space.
  • Never pour boiling water on a pipe near fixtures. Heat can crack them.

If water stays off after the pipe thaws, or you hear a hiss behind the wall, the pipe has probably split. Frozen ice often plugs the crack until it melts, so the leak may begin only when the pipe warms.

Why Minnesota Winters Split Pipes

The Minnesota DNR Twin Cities normals for 1991 to 2020 put January average lows at about 9 to 11 degrees Fahrenheit. That is the Twin Cities airport station, not Rosemount itself, but it tells you what a typical January night is like across the metro.

Pipes in outside walls, garages, crawl spaces and uninsulated rim joists feel those nights first. Know where those runs are in your house, keep cabinet doors open on cold nights, and let a faucet drip when a hard freeze is forecast.

A split pipe repair is plumbing work under the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. The state says altered parts of existing plumbing must be inspected and tested, so ask the plumber whether your repair needs an inspection and who arranges it.

Who Handles What on the Public Side

A burst on your side of the meter is a plumbing job. A main break in the street is the city's job. If you see water running down the street or a sinkhole, do not call a plumber first.

The city says after-hours water emergencies on the public side, such as a main break, go through 911. The Dakota Communications Center dispatcher alerts the proper Public Works staff. The city also says Public Works can shut off service if a plumber advises it is necessary.

If your own shutoff valve will not close, tell us. A plumber can confirm that the main valve is stuck and advise on whether a street shutoff is needed, which Public Works handles.

Repair or Replace the Damaged Section

Most bursts are fixed by replacing a short section. Whether to go further depends on what the rest of the pipe looks like.

SituationUsual approachWhy
Single split in good pipeCut out the section and replace it with new pipe and fittingsThe rest of the line is sound, so a short repair is enough.
Several splits or pinholesReplace a longer runRepeat failures show the pipe is aging or was frozen along its length.
Corroded or crusted pipeReplace the run and discuss repipingWeak pipe tends to fail again nearby.
Split at a fitting or valveReplace the fitting and test nearby jointsJoint failures can come from movement or poor soldering.
Frozen but not splitThaw, insulate and monitorNo damage means no repair, only prevention.

Drywall, Flooring and Insurance Documentation

Water damage claims go better with records. Collect these before cleanup starts.

  • ✓
    Photos and video.Capture each wet room, the broken pipe, stained ceilings and damaged belongings from several angles, with dates.
  • ✓
    A list of damaged items.Write down what was affected, where it was, and roughly when you bought it.
  • ✓
    Keep damaged materials.Keep a piece of wet drywall, trim or flooring until your insurance contact says you can discard it.
  • ✓
    Plumber's findings.Ask for a written note of where the pipe failed, what the cause appeared to be, and what was repaired.
  • ✓
    Receipts.Save receipts for emergency repairs, fans, dehumidifiers and temporary housing.

Wet drywall that stays damp can grow mold, so drying matters. Remove standing water quickly, run fans, and ask whether affected drywall needs to be cut out. We repair the plumbing. A drywall or restoration contractor handles the rest.

What Changes the Price of a Burst Pipe Repair

You hear a clear price before work starts. These factors move it up or down.

Pipe material.Copper, PEX and galvanized lines use different fittings, tools and labor.
How reachable the break is.A break in an open basement ceiling is quick. A break behind a finished wall or under a floor takes more time.
Length of the damaged run.A short section is a small job. A run damaged by freezing may need a long replacement.
After-hours timing.A call at night or on a weekend may be priced differently, and we explain that up front.

Some homeowner policies cover sudden pipe bursts but treat gradual leaks differently. Your insurer decides coverage, so ask them early and keep your plumber's notes handy.
