No Heat Tonight? Check These First

Work through these in order. Stop and call as soon as one of them points to a problem you should not touch.

  • 1
    Leave if you smell gas or a detector sounds.Carbon monoxide or a gas smell means leave the building and call from outside. Do this before any other step.
  • 2
    Check the thermostat.Make sure it is set to heat and above the room temperature. Replace dead batteries.
  • 3
    Check power to the boiler.Boilers need electricity even when they burn gas. Look at the switch near the unit and the breaker. Reset a tripped breaker once only.
  • 4
    Read the pressure gauge.Most home boilers sit in a modest range printed on the gauge or the label. A reading near zero means the boiler lost water, which usually means a leak. Do not keep adding water without finding it.
  • 5
    Note any lights, codes or sounds.Write down what the display or lights show and whether the igniter clicks, glows or stays silent. Do not relight a gas appliance yourself. Tell us what you saw.

If radiators are cold at the top but warm at the bottom, trapped air may be the cause. A plumber can bleed it safely, and we explain the risks below.

Boiler Noises and Warning Signs

Boilers talk before they fail. These are the signs we see most.

Banging or knocking.

Often air trapped in the system or a pump issue. Can also come from scale in the heat exchanger.

Kettling or rumbling.

A sound like a kettle boiling means scale or sludge is overheating the water locally. It shortens the life of the heat exchanger.

Water at the relief valve.

A relief valve that leaks or discharges can mean pressure is climbing too high. Do not cap or plug it.

Pressure keeps dropping.

A boiler that needs frequent refilling has a leak somewhere, in a joint, a radiator valve or the boiler itself.

Cold radiators.

Cold spots usually mean air, a stuck valve or a failing circulator pump.

Frequent resets.

If the boiler locks out repeatedly, there is a cause behind it, such as a flame sensor, ignition or flow problem.

Why a Cold Night in Rosemount Is Serious

January average lows at the Twin Cities airport station run about 9 to 11 degrees Fahrenheit. That is the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources 1991 to 2020 normal and not a Rosemount reading. On a night like that a house with no heat cools quickly.

The practical tie-back is that a dead boiler is also a freeze risk for pipes along outside walls and in crawl spaces. If heat will be off for hours, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and let a thin stream of water run. Call us if a pipe has already frozen, and read about burst pipe repair.

What Only a Plumber Should Open

A plumber works on the water side of a boiler: the pressure relief valve, the expansion tank, the fill valve, the circulator pump, air vents and leaks in the piping. Rosemount Express Plumbing is licensed and insured, and this is plumbing work and not heating and cooling work. Gas appliance work is done with proper care, and we tell you when a task needs the gas utility or another trade.

Owners can check the thermostat, the power switch and the gauge. Owners should not open the burner compartment, adjust a gas valve, remove the relief valve or cap a discharge pipe. Those tasks are a safety risk even when the boiler seems simple.

Repair or Replace an Aging Boiler

Age and repeat failures decide this more than any single part.

Repair makes sense when.

  • The fault is one part such as a pump, valve or sensor.
  • The heat exchanger is sound and the unit is not leaking inside.
  • Repairs have been rare.

Replacement makes sense when.

  • The heat exchanger is cracked or leaking.
  • You are paying for repeated repairs each winter.
  • The boiler cannot keep up with the house.

We tell you honestly if a repair is not worth it, and we price the options before any work.
