What changes from town to town

Water hardness is the clearest difference. City pages put Rosemount at 17 grains per gallon, Hastings at 16, Apple Valley at about 24 and Inver Grove Heights at 20 to 22. Harder water means faster scale in tank water heaters and faucet cartridges.

Sump pump rules also differ. Farmington and Inver Grove Heights ban sump pump discharge to the sanitary sewer, Hastings has a clear-water ordinance, and for Rosemount the safe move is to ask the city Building Permits and Inspections office before connecting anything.

The same state plumbing code applies in every town we serve, which is the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as Minnesota Rules chapter 4714 adopted it. What changes from town to town is the permit office and the water supply, which the town pages cover.

What to have ready when you call

A few details let the dispatcher match the visit to the problem and tell you quickly whether we can reach you.

The street address.

Include the town and ZIP code so the dispatcher knows which city rules and water supply apply.

What you see.

Water on the floor, a gurgling drain, no heat or a smell. Short and specific is best.

Where the shutoff is.

Whether you found and closed the fixture or main shutoff before calling.

Access.

Whether the area is a finished basement, a crawlspace or an outbuilding, and who can let the plumber in.
