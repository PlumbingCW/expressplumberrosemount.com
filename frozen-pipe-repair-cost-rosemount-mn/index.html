Frozen Pipe Repair Cost in Rosemount, MN: Thaw, Repair, Prevent

A frozen pipe is blocked by ice and may or may not be damaged. Thaw it slowly with gentle heat, check for leaks as it opens, and never use an open flame.

Open the tap nearest the frozen section. Then warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels. Start near the tap and work back toward the frozen spot. Never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame, because a pipe can scorch the wall and start a fire before the ice melts.

Frozen pipes cost little to fix if nothing splits. A split pipe is different, since it adds repair work and water damage. The price depends on where the pipe is, how many sections failed and how much needs to be opened.

We cannot quote a number here because every house differs, but you will get a clear price before work starts. The rest of this guide covers how to tell what you have and how to prevent it.

How to Thaw a Frozen Pipe Safely

Patience protects the pipe. Fast heat can crack a pipe that survived the freeze.

1

Find the frozen pipe.

If one tap runs and another does not, trace the line to a cold spot such as an exterior wall, a garage or a crawl space.

2

Open the tap.

Leave the faucet open so melting water has a place to go and pressure can release.

3

Apply gentle heat.

Use a hair dryer, a heating pad or towels soaked in warm water. Keep heat moving along the pipe and away from flammable material.

4

Watch for leaks as it clears.

Look at joints and the wall as water starts to flow. A drip or spray means the pipe has split.

5

Keep water flowing a while.

Leave the tap running a bit after the ice clears so any remaining slush moves out.

If you cannot reach the frozen section, such as one behind a finished wall, call a plumber instead of cutting into the wall yourself.

Never Use an Open Flame

A blowtorch, a propane heater or a kerosene heater has no place near a frozen pipe. Flames ignite wood framing and insulation, and the heat can melt solder joints or cause a steam blast inside a sealed pipe.

Do not use a space heater without supervision either. Slow, low heat is the goal, and a hair dryer in your hand is safer than a heater you walk away from.

Replacing a split section of pipe is plumbing work under the state plumbing code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. Pipes in outside walls and unheated spaces should be insulated according to the manufacturer's guidelines for the material.

Frozen or Split: How to Tell

Ice blocks a pipe, and a split lets water out. The signs differ, and the difference decides what happens next.

SignLikely frozenLikely split
Water at the tap.None or a trickle.Normal at first, then a leak appears elsewhere.
Visible pipe.Frost or a cold section.Bulge, crack or beads of water.
Sounds.Silent.Hissing, dripping or running water.
After thaw.Flow returns, no leak.Water stains, drips or pressure loss.
Wall or ceiling.Dry.Damp patches, bubbling paint or a sag.

A split pipe may stay hidden until it thaws. Check for leaks as the ice melts, and again after several hours.

If You Find a Split: Do These First

A split pipe floods fast once the ice melts. Act before you try to fix anything.

  • 1
    Shut off the main.Close the main water valve so no more water reaches the split. Know where it is before an emergency.
  • 2
    Open taps to drain the line.Opening faucets lets water drain from the broken section and relieves pressure.
  • 3
    Switch off electricity if water is near it.If water reaches outlets, fixtures or the panel, keep clear and call a plumber and an electrician.
  • 4
    Limit the damage.Move belongings, mop what you can and photograph the damage for your records.

What Drives the Price of a Frozen Pipe Repair

Thawing alone costs far less than a split repair, and a split repair varies a lot. These are the factors.

Thaw only or repair.A pipe that thaws and holds is a short visit. A split adds cutting, new pipe and testing.
Location of the damage.A pipe in an open basement ceiling is quick to reach. A pipe behind a finished wall or under a slab takes more work.
Number of splits.A cold snap can crack several sections. Each one has to be found and fixed.
Pipe material.Copper, plastic and older pipe each need different fittings and methods.
Water damage and drying.Wet drywall, insulation and flooring may need removal. That work is separate from the plumbing repair.
Time and hour of the call.Same-day and after-hours work can differ from a scheduled visit. We explain it up front.

Prevention Before the Cold Arrives

Most frozen pipes are preventable with a little work in the fall and a few habits in the cold.

Insulate exposed pipes.

Wrap pipes in garages, crawl spaces and along exterior walls with foam sleeves. Seal gaps where cold air reaches the pipes.

Let a tap drip.

On the coldest nights, a slow drip from a tap on an exterior wall keeps water moving.

Open cabinet doors.

Open the doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm room air reaches the pipes.

Keep the heat on.

Do not let the house go cold, even when you are away. A low thermostat setting costs less than a burst pipe.

Disconnect hoses.

Remove outdoor hoses and shut off and drain outdoor faucets before freezing weather.

Know where your main water shutoff is, and show everyone in the house. That one habit limits the damage when something goes wrong.

Hidden Leaks After a Freeze

A pipe can split inside a wall and show no sign for days. Watch for rising water bills, damp spots, a musty smell or the sound of running water when everything is off.

Our leak detection service finds hidden leaks without tearing open every wall. Finding the exact spot keeps repairs small.

If you find a leak that you cannot place, close the main valve and call. Water behind a wall does damage even when you cannot see it.

Do It Yourself or Call a Plumber

Some freezes are simple. Others are a plumber's job from the start.

Reasonable DIY.

  • A single exposed pipe you can reach.
  • No sign of a leak as it thaws.
  • Hair dryer or heating pad only.
  • Shutoff valve works and is easy to reach.

Call a plumber.

  • Pipe behind a wall, floor or ceiling.
  • Any leak, spray or bulging pipe.
  • Multiple frozen lines at once.
  • A main valve that will not close.
