How to pick the right page

Start with what you can see or hear. Water on the floor points to a leak or a failed pump, a gurgle in several drains points to the main line, and a cold house points to the boiler. If more than one fits, call and describe it, because the order of the fixes matters.

Hot water trouble is the most common call, so the water heater pages are split by situation. Read water heater repair if the unit is still working in part, replacement if the tank leaks or has rusted through, and installation if you are planning a move or an upgrade.

Every service listed here follows the state plumbing standards in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and the pages say where a city permit or inspection may come into play.

Drains, sewers and the line you own

Clogs get a different answer depending on where they sit. A single slow sink is a drain cleaning job, a backup in the lowest floor drain points to sewer line cleaning, and a line that keeps failing after cleaning needs a camera inspection before anyone talks about repair.

In Rosemount the homeowner owns the private pipe from the house to the street main, and the city maintains the main. Knowing that boundary keeps you from paying for a fix on the wrong side of the property line.

Winter, water and the basement

Minnesota winters drive a lot of plumbing work. Frozen supply lines turn into burst pipe repairs, boilers that quit need boiler repair the same day, and spring thaw tests every sump pump in the neighborhood.

Planned work fits the calendar too. Summer and fall are the easier seasons for repiping, plumbing installation and basement waterproofing, when the house is not in the middle of a cold snap.
