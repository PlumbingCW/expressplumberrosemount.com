Leak or backup at your business: what to do right now

A calm first ten minutes limits the damage. Walk through these before we arrive.

  • 1
    Find and close the nearest shutoff.A leaking fixture usually has its own stop valve. If it will not close, shut the main building valve and tell staff which areas have no water.
  • 2
    Stop using the affected fixtures.Do not flush restrooms or run sinks, dishwashers or floor sinks if the drain is backing up. Added water makes the flooding worse.
  • 3
    Cut power where water is near equipment.Switch off circuits to wet walk-in coolers, kitchen equipment and outlets at the panel. Do not stand in water to do it.
  • 4
    Protect stock and records.Move product, paper files and electronics off the floor. Photograph the damage for your own records and your insurer.
  • 5
    Call the 24/7 line.Tell us the building type, where the water is and what you shut off. We will say whether it needs a visit tonight or can wait for a same-day appointment when available.

If a drain backup brings sewage into a food area, close that area to customers and staff until it is cleaned and checked. Wet restrooms with standing water can also be a slip risk, so use cones or tape.

How a commercial call differs from a home call

A shop or restaurant has more fixtures, longer pipe runs and bigger water heaters than a house. A problem in one restroom can affect a whole row of fixtures, and a floor drain may serve several rooms. We ask who was open, what was running and what changed before the problem started.

Business owners also work against the clock. We tell you early whether a fix can be finished in one visit or needs parts, so you can plan staffing and customers. Commercial buildings often have tenants, landlords and managers, so we will explain who needs to approve what before work starts.

Commercial plumbing in Minnesota follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as Minnesota adopted it in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and that code addresses backflow protection and grease interceptors. Ask which devices at your building need periodic testing, and keep the paperwork where you can find it.

What goes wrong in restaurants, shops, offices and light industrial buildings

The building type shapes the failures. This is what we see most.

Restaurants and kitchens.

Grease-laden drains back up through floor sinks and prep sinks. Dishwasher lines, hand sinks and water heaters take a beating from constant use.

Retail shops.

Slow restroom drains, leaking supply lines and water heaters in back rooms are the usual calls. A leak above displays costs more than the repair.

Offices.

Restroom fixtures run constantly. Running toilets, leaking faucets and clogged floor drains are common, and they often go unreported until a ceiling stains.

Light industrial buildings.

Floor drains, hose bibs, shop sinks and larger water heaters see hard use. Debris, oil and sediment foul drains faster than in an office.

Floor drains need a trap that holds water. A dry trap lets sewer gas into the building, so we also check that drains in rarely used rooms are primed.

Backflow prevention and testing

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops water from flowing backward from the building into the public water supply. Many commercial buildings have one on the main water line, on a fire line or on equipment such as boilers, carbonated drinks machines and irrigation. If one fails, dirty water can be drawn into clean lines.

These devices generally have to be tested periodically, and a failed test means repair or replacement. We do not state a schedule here, because the schedule that applies depends on your building. Ask the City of Rosemount's Building Permits and Inspections office what applies to your address, then call us for the test or repair.

A preventer that drips from its relief port, has been bumped by a forklift or is exposed to freezing needs attention soon. Our plumbing installation page covers new devices for a tenant fit-out.

Grease traps and interceptors

A grease trap or interceptor catches fats, oil and food solids from kitchen drains before they reach the building's sewer line. It has to be emptied and cleaned on a regular schedule. When it is full, grease passes through and coats the pipe walls downstream.

That coating narrows the line until a backup follows, often at the dinner rush. Backups usually show first at the floor sink nearest the lowest drain. Clearing the line with hydro jetting removes the buildup, but the cause is a trap that is full, undersized or missed on the maintenance list.

We can look at the trap, explain what you see and tell you whether the line past it needs cleaning. Regular pumping costs less than an emergency call on a Friday night.

Storm drains outside your building and the drains you own

The City of Rosemount asks property owners to keep storm drains clear of leaves, snow, ice and other debris. Water in storm drains flows untreated to lakes, ponds and wetlands, so do not wash mop water, oil or grease into a storm inlet. If the drain near your building floods the lot, report it to the city's Stormwater Specialist at 651-322-2075.

Storm drains are separate from the sanitary sewer inside your building. The city runs the public sewer main, and property owners are responsible for backups in the private service line. A floor drain backup inside the building is your line to clear, and we can help with that through sewer line cleaning and sewer line repair.

Source: City of Rosemount, Stormwater and Sanitary Sewer pages.

What drives the price of commercial plumbing work

A shop, a restaurant and a warehouse are very different jobs, so we do not publish figures. These factors move the price.

Building type and size.More fixtures and longer pipe runs add time. A restaurant with several floor sinks takes longer to trace than a small office.
Access and hours.Work above a ceiling or around equipment takes longer. A call after hours is scoped differently from a same-day appointment when available.
Equipment involved.Larger water heaters, backflow assemblies and interceptors require more parts and handling than residential fixtures.
What the line needs.Light cleaning is quick. A jetter or a camera takes longer, and repair of a failed line is a separate job.

You hear the price before work starts, with the scope explained, so you can approve it with your manager or landlord. If an insurer is involved, photographs and a written scope help.
