Old Hastings Streets and Newer Subdivisions Fail Differently

The city's housing plan describes a compact grid of streets around downtown that held until the 1950s, then suburban curving streets and cul-de-sacs. That split shows up in the pipes.

Older grid neighborhoods.

  • Most pre-1960 housing sits east of Pine Street and north of 10th Street, per the city plan.
  • Older drain lines may be clay or cast iron, which can crack or shift.
  • Older water lines may be galvanized steel, which can narrow with age.
  • The city has permit pages for changes to historic properties, so ask before exterior work.

Newer subdivisions.

  • The plan says 23 percent of homes were built in the years after 1999.
  • Plastic and copper are more likely, though we check each house.
  • Problems lean toward failed fittings, water heaters and sump pumps.
  • Fewer surprises under the floor, but a camera look is still worth it.

The same plan says 11 percent of housing was built before 1940 and 31.1 percent before 1960, using 2016 survey data. That is a real share of old housing. When we hear an address east of Pine Street, we plan for an older house.

Sewer Trouble in an Older Hastings House

Hastings Public Works and the city's sewer districts handle the public system, and flow ends at the Metropolitan Council treatment plant in town. The pipe from your house to the city main is the owner's responsibility. If the blockage or break is on that stretch, the repair and the bill are yours.

In a house with an older line, repeated backups usually mean the pipe itself has a problem, such as roots, a belly or a joint that has shifted. Cleaning clears the symptom, but it does not fix a broken pipe. A camera shows which one you have, and the answer decides whether you need sewer line repair or only a cleaning.

The city has also advertised a sewer repair grant program for private lines. Ask the city for current terms before you plan around it, since funds and rules can change. The program described a video inspection showing damage as part of the process, so keep any camera footage we give you.

Plumbing work in Hastings follows the UPC-based code in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. The city states that plumbing permits are required for water softeners, water heaters and water line replacement, which is useful to know before you hire.

Backed Up or Leaking Right Now: First Steps

Do these while you wait for the call back. They limit damage and give the plumber a better picture.

  • 1
    Shut off the main water.If a supply line is leaking, close the main valve. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • 2
    Stop sending water down.No toilets, no washer, no showers. In a sewer backup, every drain in the house feeds the problem.
  • 3
    Mind the electricity.If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out of the wet area. Call 911 if you think there is a shock risk.
  • 4
    Photograph the damage.Take photos before you move anything. Your insurer will want them.
  • 5
    Call the 24/7 line.Tell us the street, the fixtures affected, and whether the main is off. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 from outside.

Galvanized Lines and the Repipe Question

Hastings counts 2 lead, 145 galvanized and 970 unknown service lines in its 2025 inventory, and runs a lead line replacement program. That leaves most lines as non-lead, but the unknown group is large. Older neighborhoods are where the unknowns and galvanized lines tend to cluster.

The city's inventory covers the pipe from the main to your house. Pipe inside the house is yours, and in an older home it can be galvanized too. Low pressure, rusty water or leaks that keep returning in several places are the signs that point toward repiping as an option. We will tell you when a repair makes sense and when replacing a run of pipe is the better value.

If the leak is on the line from the street, water service line repair and replacement is the service to read. A replacement can trigger a permit under the city's rules.
