This page is for planned work. Think of a new heater in a remodel, a heater moved to a better spot, a fuel switch or an upgrade you have put off. If your tank is already leaking in Rosemount, start with our page on emergency water heater replacement.

A good installation is mostly about the details around the tank. Venting, a drain pan, a safe discharge line, an expansion tank where needed and proper support decide whether the heater runs well for the long haul.

Start with what you want to change

Every installation begins with one question: is this a like-for-like swap, or are you changing something? A like-for-like swap uses the existing gas line, circuit, vent and water connections. A change adds work that needs to be planned.

Moving the heater means new water lines, a new vent path or a new electrical run. Switching fuel means a new gas line or a new circuit. Adding an expansion tank or a drain pan is a smaller change, but it still needs space and fittings.

The best time to decide is before the old unit comes out. A short phone call about your house, your fuel and your plans lets us explain the scope and give a clear price before work starts.

Water heater installs are plumbing work under the Minnesota Plumbing Code in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, with the relief valve discharge, expansion control and venting set by that code and the manufacturer. Ask the city Building Permits and Inspections office whether your swap needs a permit, since permit requirements are set locally.

Decisions to settle before install day

A few choices shape the whole job. Settle them early and the visit goes quickly.

  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓

Venting, drain pan, discharge line and expansion tank

These are the parts people do not see once the heater is in, and they are the parts that matter most when something goes wrong.

Venting.

On a gas unit, the vent carries combustion gases outside. It must slope properly, stay tight and draw correctly. We check it as part of every gas install.

Drain pan.

A pan under the tank catches a slow leak and sends it to a safe place instead of the floor. It helps most when the heater sits above finished space.

Discharge line.

The relief valve needs a pipe that carries hot water or steam to a safe spot if it ever opens. This pipe must never be capped or blocked.

Expansion tank.

Heating water makes it expand. A tank on the supply absorbs that pressure so it does not strain the heater and the fixtures.

Support and clearance.

The tank sits level, with room for service and for airflow where the unit needs it. A stable, level base matters more than any accessory.

Code compliance in general terms means the install follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code and any local rules. Your city office confirms the local side.

Install day from dry fit to final test

A planned install follows a steady order, and most of the care goes into the final checks.

1

Walk the site.

The plumber confirms the location, the fuel connection, the vent path and the water lines, and agrees on the plan with you.

2

Remove the old unit.

Water is shut off, the tank is drained and disconnected, and the old unit is hauled away.

3

Set and connect the new heater.

The tank goes on a level base with a pan. New connectors, a relief valve, a discharge line and any expansion tank go in.

4

Vent, fill and start.

On gas, the vent is checked before the unit is lit. The tank is filled with air purged from the lines, then power or gas is restored.

5

Test and explain.

Every joint is checked for leaks, the temperature is set and verified at a tap, and the plumber shows you the shutoff valves and the controls.

Hire a licensed plumbing contractor in Minnesota

In Minnesota, a business that contracts plumbing work must hold a plumbing contractor license from the Department of Labor and Industry. The license requires a responsible master plumber, insurance and a bond. Rosemount Express Plumbing is licensed and insured.

You can check any contractor yourself. The Department of Labor and Industry has a license and registration lookup on its website, and you can search a name or a license type there. Ask any plumber you are considering to explain who will do the work and who is responsible for it.

State law also allows owners to do plumbing work in their own occupied home, unless a local ordinance forbids it. Gas, venting and hot water are areas where a mistake is costly, so many owners leave them to a licensed contractor.

If you are also planning other fixture or pipe changes, our page on plumbing installation covers that side.

What drives the price of an installation

We give you a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that set the number.

Scope of change.A like-for-like swap costs less than a move or a fuel switch because the existing connections are reused.
Unit type and size.A larger tank, a power-vented model or a sealed-combustion unit costs more to buy and install.
Venting and gas work.A new vent path, new gas piping or a gas line upsizing adds materials and labor.
Electrical work.A new circuit or a panel upgrade for an electric unit is its own line of work.
Code items.A drain pan, discharge line and expansion tank are added where needed, and old fittings that fail inspection get replaced.
Access and permits.A tight space, long pipe runs or a permit fee from the city, where one applies, changes the total.
